The Small Holding

A fictional regional bottle shop. The voice is drier and shorter than Tidewell — written for a counter where people will tell you what they’re cooking.

A short list, on purpose.

The Small Holding is a bottle shop that only puts a wine on the shelf if we would take it home on a Tuesday. The list is small. It changes. We’d rather run out than pad it.

How we buy

We taste it. We ask what it cost to make, not just what it costs us. We don’t take a wine because a supplier has a volume target, and we don’t write tasting notes that could sit on any other bottle in the shop.

If you know exactly what you want, say so. If you don’t, tell us what you’re cooking, and how much you actually want to spend. The second number is as useful as the first.

This week’s twelve

A standing section, updated weekly: six under $30, four to sit with dinner, two worth opening for a reason. Prices on the bottles. No “market price”.

A note on scores

We don’t print them. A 94 from someone you’ve never cooked for is not more useful than a sentence about whether it can handle a roast chicken.

Tasting notes

2023 Semillon, Hunter Valley — $26

Lemon pith and a bit of toast, even this young. Built for a plate of prawns, or the fridge door on a hot afternoon. Will be more interesting in three years, if you can leave it alone.

2022 Grenache, McLaren Vale — $29

Red fruit, not jam. A little dry herb. This is a Tuesday stew wine, and that is a compliment. Serve it slightly cool.

2021 Cabernet, Frankland River — $42

Cassia, blackcurrant, a proper tannin. Open it when you have a couple of hours and something with a bone in it. Not a “smooth” wine, and we didn’t try to sell it as one.

NV Sparkling, Tasmania — $34

Clean, dry, more apple than cake. The bottle we put on the counter when someone says “just something to walk in with”. It won’t embarrass you, and it won’t try to be Champagne.

Weekly email

Subject: Six under $30, and one to open on Saturday · Preview: The list is twelve this week. We ran out of the riesling.

Hello,

The riesling went in two days. That’s our fault for putting twelve bottles on the floor and then talking about it. What’s in this week:

A Hunter semillon at $26 that wants prawns, or just a cold glass. A McLaren Vale grenache at $29 for a stew — serve it cooler than you think. One Tasmanian sparkling at $34, which is the “walk in with this” bottle if you need one.

If you’re coming in on Saturday and you want something for a roast, ask for the Frankland cabernet. We’ll open the tasting bottle.

If the emails are too many, leave the list. The shop will still be here.

— The Small Holding
