Tidewell

A fictional skincare line for Australian weather. I wrote the words, designed the mark, and made the stills. Nothing here is for sale, and no real shop hired me to do it.

Homepage

Made for the kind of day that starts in air-conditioning and ends in a nor’easter.

Three bottles. That’s the routine. A cleanser, a moisturiser, a daily SPF — each one made to do a real job on Australian skin, and nothing else.

The problem we actually started with

Most “coastal” skincare smells like a candle shop and promises a holiday. Tidewell started from a smaller, less photogenic problem: skin that goes tight at three in the afternoon, then raw on a weekend walk along a New South Wales foreshore.

If your bathroom shelf is already full, we are not trying to add a fourth step. We make three products, in small batches. If something can’t earn its place, we don’t make it.

Saltwash Gel Cleanser

120 ml · $42

A low-foam gel that takes off sunscreen and city dust without that tight, squeaky feeling people still mistake for “clean”.

The base is a mild sugar surfactant, not a stripping sulphate. There’s sea celery extract in it, and nothing you’d call a fragrance — your bathroom will not smell like a beach shop. Use it once, at night. In the morning, unless you’ve been swimming or sweating, water is enough.

How to use

Wet your face. A pea-sized amount, worked in for twenty seconds, is plenty. Rinse. If your skin feels tight afterwards, you’re using too much, or using it twice a day when you don’t need to.

What’s in it, in plain language

Water, a mild sugar-based cleanser, glycerin, sea celery extract, a touch of squalane so it doesn’t dry you out, and a preservative so it stays safe in a steamy bathroom. The full INCI list is on the carton. No essential oils, no beads, no “detox”.

Good for normal to combination skin, and for people who wear sunscreen every day. If you have a skin condition, or you’re under the care of a dermatologist, ask them first. This is a cleanser, not a treatment.

Harbour Cream

50 ml · $58

A moisturiser that earns its place on a shelf that’s already too crowded.

It is meant to last the afternoon — through office air-conditioning, a windy walk to the car, and the dry heat that sits over a lot of Australian inland towns in January. It sinks in. It doesn’t leave a film. It doesn’t pill under sunscreen, which is the only reason a lot of “nice” moisturisers get abandoned by Wednesday.

The main work is done by shea, squalane, and a light ceramide. There is no fragrance, and no glitter of any kind.

How to use

A small amount, on a clean, slightly damp face, morning and night. Wait a minute before SPF. If it pills, you’ve used too much, or you haven’t let it settle.

Shoreline SPF 50

80 ml · $46

A daily mineral sunscreen that looks like skin. It uses zinc oxide, at a level that actually meets SPF 50, and it is ground fine enough that you shouldn’t need a “trick” to rub the white away. There is a sheer tint in one honest shade, and a clear version if you’d rather. Neither is a foundation. Neither will even out a late night.

Two-finger lengths for face and neck. Reapply after a swim, a heavy sweat, or two hours outside. A moisturiser with “SPF 15” in the morning is not a substitute.

A straight note: no sunscreen is “reef safe” just because a label would like it to be, and we don’t print that. If you have very deep skin, try the clear version first and see it in daylight — mineral tints can still leave a cast, and we’d rather you knew that here than on a Saturday morning.

From the journal: why we don’t make a toner

If you already own a toner, this is not an argument for throwing it out. It is an argument for why we didn’t make you a new one.

Toner comes from a time when soap left a film, and a splash of something astringent was supposed to finish the job. Most modern cleansers don’t leave that film. What a lot of toners do now is add water, a little glycerin, sometimes an acid, and a smell. That can be pleasant. It is rarely the thing standing between you and comfortable skin.

If your skin feels tight after cleansing, the first thing to look at is the cleanser, not a bottle that comes after it. If your skin feels fine, and you like the pause of a toner, keep using the one you have. We just won’t sell you a second.

The useful exceptions are the treatments that happen to come in a toner bottle: a prescribed acid, something your dermatologist actually asked you to use. Those aren’t toners in the old sense. They’re treatments. We don’t make those either. That’s a different, more careful kind of work, and it shouldn’t come from a small batch shop with three products and a website.

What we would rather you do with the space on the shelf is simpler. Clean at night. Moisturise. Wear a proper SPF in the day. If there’s a fourth thing in your cabinet you can truthfully explain, it can stay. If you can’t, it can probably go, whether we sell a replacement or not.
