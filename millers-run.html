Miller’s Run

A one-person espresso cart at a Saturday livestock market in a New South Wales regional town. I wrote the brief so I could show how I think about a mark — no paid work was done for a real cart of this name.

The owner makes coffee properly, doesn’t do flavoured syrups, and wants a mark that feels like a painted farm gate, not a Melbourne café that has been “inspired by the country”. It has to work as a stamp on a takeaway cup, in one colour on a corflute sign that will sit in a paddock, and small, on a card.

What to avoid: clip-art wheat, a cow, a coffee bean wearing a hat, a monogram so thin it disappears at 20 millimetres, and the word “artisan”.

Concept 1 — the gate wordmark (recommended)

The name set in a sturdy serif, the two words stacked, with a single horizontal bar running through the middle like a gate rail. No picture. The bar is the idea, and it also solves the practical problem: at a distance, you still read a strong, simple shape.

This is the one I would take to a real cart. It is the easiest to stamp, the least likely to date, and it doesn’t rely on a cute drawing of a mill that the town may or may not have.

Concept 2 — the market badge

A circular badge, thick outer ring, the name on the curve, “Saturday coffee” in small caps at the foot, and a simple M in the centre drawn with the same rail logic as the wordmark. Better for a sticker, slightly busier on a cup. I would use this as a secondary mark, not the only one.

Concept 3 — the single-line stamp

“Miller’s Run” in spaced sans-serif capitals, like a stock brand, with a short rule above and below. Very direct. A little severe for a coffee cart, but it would be honest on a navy work shirt. If the owner wanted something warmer, I would go back to Concept 1 rather than decorate this one.

Recommendation

Lead with Concept 1, in one colour: a dark oak brown or a straight black, on unbleached cups. Do not add a tagline on the cup. “Coffee on Saturdays” can sit on the corflute sign, in a smaller sans, underneath.

A short note on process, if a client asked: I would still want to stand in that market for an hour before locking the colour, because dust and paddock light are less kind than a screen. The drawing can be finished before that. The colour shouldn’t be.
